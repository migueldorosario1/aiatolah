OpenAI’s finance team just revealed a remarkably practical way to turn a dusty forecast spreadsheet into a living, breathing decision tool — and it takes just a few clicks inside ChatGPT Work. No coding. No expensive dashboard software. Just a link to your Excel file and a short conversation with the AI.

Kyle, a member of OpenAI’s strategic finance group, walks through a workflow that will feel familiar to anyone who has spent late nights re‑crunching numbers for leadership. Traditionally, after building a detailed financial model, you’d manually translate it into slide decks, then repeat the whole exercise every time an executive asked “what if we change this assumption?” ChatGPT Work collapses that loop: it can ingest a spreadsheet, understand the business context, and instantly generate an interactive web site — something between a presentation and a lightweight app — where stakeholders can play with scenarios, listen to an audio briefing, and see real‑time impact without touching the original model.

The process is aggressively simple. You open the ChatGPT desktop app (or web), switch to the Work module, drop the spreadsheet link, and feed the AI some product context. You then tell it who the review is for, what decision rests on the numbers, and the deadline. From there, you ask it to build a site that lets leaders toggle assumptions, compare forecasts, and even hear a spoken summary. Finally, you audit the outputs against the source model before giving the green light. The result is a CFO‑proof sandbox that feels like a live dashboard but starts life as a plain Excel file.

What makes this noteworthy isn’t just the convenience — it’s how it closes the gap between the raw computation of a spreadsheet and the fluid, conversational way humans actually make decisions. In many ways, it mirrors a broader shift across the AI industry. Chinese research labs like DeepSeek and Qwen have been racing to embed similar reasoning capabilities directly into productivity tools, while Kimi (from Moonshot AI) famously attacked the long‑context problem so that entire documents can become interactive. At the hardware level, this trend is driving demand for inference‑efficient chips that can spin up such agents without hogging cloud resources — a space where Chinese chipmakers are quietly accelerating alongside global giants.

While OpenAI’s demo focuses on finance, the pattern is universal: any team that lives inside spreadsheets — from supply chain to HR planning — can now turn a static model into a collaborative, AI‑hosted micro‑app. The era of the interactive spreadsheet is arriving, and it looks a lot less like a grid of cells and more like a conversation.
